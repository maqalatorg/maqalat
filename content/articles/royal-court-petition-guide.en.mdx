A Royal Court petition (ma'rood) is a formal letter that a citizen or resident sends to the King's Court or the Crown Prince's Court to request help on a case where the normal administrative process has been exhausted. The Court doesn't rule on the case itself — it forwards the case to the competent authority with a recommendation for careful review. Drafting the petition well multiplies the chance of a response, and sending it through the correct channel prevents it being lost.

## When to submit a petition

A petition is not a substitute for the standard procedures — it is a step **after they have been exhausted**. Cases typically accepted:

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Urgent humanitarian | Medical costs, family in hardship, housing lost after disaster |
| Pardon request | Prisoner pardon, sentence reduction, private-right waiver |
| Administrative complaint | Government decision after appeal and litigation |
| National suggestion | Projects, legislation, government services |
| Aid request | Employment for people with disabilities, help for a prisoner's family |

**Not accepted**: cases pending court judgment, private banking matters, individual commercial disputes, standard employment requests.

## Correct petition structure

The petition follows a standardised format. Any deviation causes the Court to return or ignore it.

```
                                                                    Date: __ / __ / 144_ AH

              His Majesty King [Name], may God protect him
              (or: His Royal Highness the Crown Prince)

              Peace be upon you, and God's mercy and blessings. And thereafter:

              From: [Full four-part name]
              ID number: [10 digits]
              Nationality: [Saudi / Resident]
              Address: [District — City]
              Mobile: [+9665…]

              Subject: [Very short title, e.g., Request for medical support for my mother]

              [Body: 3 to 5 paragraphs only]
              Para 1: Brief background (who you are, current situation).
              Para 2: The case itself in brief chronological order.
              Para 3: Formal steps you've taken and their outcome (with reference numbers where possible).
              Para 4: A single clear request in one sentence.
              Para 5: Thanks, prayers, and request for consideration.

              Attachments:
              — ID copy.
              — Supporting documents (medical reports, decisions, correspondence).

              Peace be upon you, and God's mercy and blessings.
              [Signature]
              [Name]
```

## Drafting rules

- **Formal, understated language**: no rhyming prose, no long poetic introductions. The Court processes thousands of petitions monthly; clarity wins.
- **Facts with numbers**: instead of "the illness is severe," write "KFSH Hospital report no. 2026-45678 confirms the diagnosis of…"
- **One clear request**: don't combine multiple asks in one petition. One case, one petition.
- **Brevity**: two pages maximum. Long petitions are skimmed.
- **Respectful without pleading**: respect is required; excessive begging weakens the letter.
- **No accusations**: if the petition is against an authority, cite the action and the reference number — avoid personal descriptions of employees.

## Official submission channels

| Channel | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Royal Court e-portal** | [royalcourt.gov.sa](https://www.royalcourt.gov.sa/) — "Communications & Petitions" section | Fastest, best documented — gives a case number immediately on submission |
| **Government 'Tawasul' portal** | [tawasul.moe.gov.sa](https://tawasul.moe.gov.sa/) — select "Royal Court" as recipient | Useful if you are outside Riyadh |
| **Official mail — Riyadh** | Royal Court headquarters, Riyadh (PO Box officially published) | Accepted but slower to process |
| **Personal visit** | Court building — Riyadh, by appointment | Rare cases only — don't rely on this |

**Important warning**: "Royal Court WhatsApp" or "direct Crown Prince number" circulated in WhatsApp groups are **not official** and are often scams. Only use the channels listed above.

## Tracking the petition

After submission, you'll receive a case number. Track it from the same platform where you submitted. Typical stages:

1. **Received**: reached the Court.
2. **Under review**: assessed by the petitions department.
3. **Referred to competent authority**: the ministry, agency, or governorate is notified to review.
4. **Final response**: comes from the competent authority via its own portal or SMS.

The Court does not rule directly; the final answer comes from the authority to which the case was referred. If you receive an unsatisfactory response, you may re-petition with **new documents** — not just a repeat of the same letter.

## Common mistakes that waste your effort

- **Emotional phrasing**: sentimental language weakens the document.
- **Petition without ID or reference number**: discarded.
- **Sending the same petition on multiple channels simultaneously**: flagged as duplicate and rejected.
- **Combining multiple requests**: each case needs its own petition.
- **Poor-quality attachments**: unclear scans cause the file to be skipped.
- **Promising documents "later"**: attach everything at once.

## Alternatives to Consider Before a Royal Court Petition

Before turning to the Royal Court, there are dedicated channels that are shorter and more effective for specific case types. A Royal Court petition is used after exhausting these channels:

| Case type | Most appropriate channel first |
|---|---|
| Administrative decision you want to appeal | [Board of Grievances](https://www.bog.gov.sa/) — Administrative Court |
| Dispute between citizen and company | [Ministry of Commerce](https://mci.gov.sa/) or Commercial Court |
| Labor case | [Qiwa](/en/qiwa-platform-guide) then Labor Court |
| Human rights violation | [Human Rights Commission](https://www.hrc.gov.sa/) |
| Administrative or financial corruption | [Control and Anti-Corruption Authority (Nazaha)](https://www.nazaha.gov.sa/) |
| Health case | [Ministry of Health](https://www.moh.gov.sa/) — patient rights complaint |
| Educational case | [Ministry of Education Tawasul](https://tawasul.moe.gov.sa/) |
| Insurance case | [Central Bank (SAMA)](https://www.sama.gov.sa/) for insurance complaint |

The Royal Court values your petition more when it sees you've tried these channels seriously — your documentation supports your petition.

## The Crown Prince Court and Its Specific Petitions

The Crown Prince Court has an independent track (though the Royal Court remains the mother entity) via [the Crown Prince's official channels](https://www.spa.gov.sa/) and the government's official digital channels. The Crown Prince's track specifically receives:

- **Vision 2030 initiatives**: suggestions for improving projects.
- **Youth causes**: entrepreneurship, employment, scholarships.
- **Technology and innovation causes**: tech projects, requests for talent support.
- **Humanitarian cases with national impact**.

The practical difference with the King's Court: the Crown Prince's track is faster in handling technical and strategic cases, while humanitarian, family, and legal cases are handled more specialized at the King's Court.

## Can I Write the Petition with a Lawyer's Help?

Yes, and it's recommended for complex legal cases. A lawyer licensed by the [Saudi Bar Association](https://www.sba.org.sa/) can:

- Draft the document in correct legal language.
- Reference statutory texts supporting your request.
- Organize attachments and case numbers logically.
- Attach judicial precedents if there's a similar ruling in your favor.

But **the petition's final signature** must be yours personally — the court prefers the direct message from its owner. The lawyer's role is advisory, not substitutive.

## What If the Petition Is Rejected?

Rejection from the Royal Court doesn't mean closing the door. Available options:

1. **Resubmit with new documents**: if new documents emerged, attach them with reference to the previous petition number.
2. **Turn to the Human Rights Commission**: if the case is rights-related, via [hrc.gov.sa](https://www.hrc.gov.sa/).
3. **Administrative Court**: for administrative decisions, via [Board of Grievances](https://www.bog.gov.sa/).
4. **Royal amnesty on occasions**: for criminal cases, submitted before national and religious holidays.

Patience is a key part — some cases took months before a response.
