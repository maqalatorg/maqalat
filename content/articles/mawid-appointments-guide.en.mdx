"Mawid" was the first digital service launched by the Saudi Ministry of Health (2017) for booking primary-care and government hospital appointments. With the later launch of [Sehhaty](/en/sehhaty-app-complete-guide), Mawid became a specialised booking arm — the two apps complement each other. URL: [mawid.moh.gov.sa](https://mawid.moh.gov.sa/).

## When to use Mawid vs Sehhaty

| Need | Better app |
|---|---|
| Urgent primary-care booking | Mawid or Sehhaty — either |
| Following up medical file | Sehhaty |
| Dispensing an e-prescription | Sehhaty (integrated Wasfaty) |
| Major hospital bookings (KFSH, National Guard, KAH) | Each hospital has an additional portal |
| Follow-up after referral | Sehhaty or Mawid |
| Vaccination certificates | Sehhaty |
| Health insurance and rights | Sehhaty |

**Practically**: many users use Sehhaty only, and it's sufficient. Mawid stays as a backup when Sehhaty is unstable, or for users who prefer a simpler interface.

## Booking steps from Mawid

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Download the "Mawid" app or open [mawid.moh.gov.sa](https://mawid.moh.gov.sa/) |
| 2 | Sign in with National Single Sign-On or Absher |
| 3 | Select "Appointments" → "New Booking" |
| 4 | Choose the service type (primary care, dental, paediatrics, etc.) |
| 5 | Pick your centre or search for another |
| 6 | Choose doctor and date/time from available slots |
| 7 | Review and confirm — booking number and QR appear |
| 8 | Confirmation notification on your mobile |

## Reschedule or cancel an appointment

- **Reschedule**: from the app → Appointments → select the appointment → "Reschedule" → pick an alternative date. Rescheduling is allowed up to 4 hours before the appointment.
- **Cancel**: same path, choose "Cancel." Please cancel at least a day before to free the slot for someone else.

**Note**: 3 consecutive no-shows lock booking for 7 days.

## Attending your appointment

- **10 minutes before**: that's what's required.
- **QR code in the app**: opens the electronic gate at reception if automated.
- **ID card**: may be requested at human reception.
- **Active Sehhaty account**: some centres link reception to your account.

## Picking the best primary-care centre

The primary-care centre is your gateway to the entire health system. Choosing well saves time and effort:

| Criterion | Why |
|---|---|
| Proximity to home | Reduces fatigue on repeated visits |
| Operating hours | Some centres are evening, some 24/7 |
| Available specialties | Paediatrics, dental, family — not every centre offers all |
| Booking pressure | New-district centres are quieter than downtown |
| Medication availability | The centre's own pharmacy beats relying on external |

## Common issues and fixes

| Issue | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No appointments in specialty | All booked | Try two weeks out or a different centre |
| "Care centre not set" | You didn't pick | From Settings, choose your centre first |
| "Mobile mismatch" | Not updated in Absher | Update Absher first |
| Appointment cancelled without reason | Reception system error | Call the centre; re-book |
| Not receiving notifications | Disabled in phone settings | Enable for the app |

## Specialty hospital appointments

Some major hospitals (KFSH, King Khalid University, National Guard…) have additional booking portals — accessed via referral from primary care through Mawid or Sehhaty. Don't book directly there unless you're already a follow-up patient.

## Tips to save time

- **Monday and Wednesday mornings are usually lighter**: many book on Sunday.
- **Confirm before going**: the doctor sometimes cancels for emergencies — you'll get a notice.
- **Prepare documents beforehand**: previous tests, prescriptions, imaging reports — don't rely solely on the electronic record.
- **Dental slots are short**: arrive early — often 30 minutes total.

## Mawid and Saudi Arabia's Health Referral System

The core of Saudi Arabia's public healthcare model is a **tiered referral system**: start at a primary healthcare centre, then be referred as needed to Tier 2 (general hospitals), then Tier 3 (specialty hospitals like King Faisal Specialist). Mawid serves Tier 1 fully, and your referrals then flow automatically through the [Ehala platform](https://ehala.moh.gov.sa/) — the backend infrastructure that moves your file between tiers.

Practically: when your primary-care doctor refers you through Ehala, you don't book the hospital yourself — the appointment appears automatically in your app days later with a specific reference number. This is the **only loop** for entering the major hospitals under government coverage.

## Mawid's Link to Health Clusters

Since 2021, the [Ministry of Health](https://www.moh.gov.sa/) began the **Health Clusters** model, which groups hospitals and primary healthcare centres geographically under unified management. This affected Mawid in two ways:

1. **Free booking within the cluster**: you're no longer restricted to a single primary healthcare centre — you can book any centre within your cluster.
2. **Data flow**: test results at the primary centre appear directly to the doctor at the cluster's hospital without needing to print them.

Each Saudi administrative region has one or more clusters (see the [official clusters list](https://www.moh.gov.sa/HealthClusters/)): Riyadh First and Second and Third Cluster, Jeddah Cluster, Makkah Cluster, Madinah Cluster, Eastern Cluster… etc. Find your cluster from Sehhaty or Mawid → "My Health Info" → "Health Cluster."

## Does Mawid Work for Expats?

Yes — expats in Saudi Arabia use Mawid with the same privileges, **provided** they're under the umbrella of **Cooperative Health Insurance** (employer insurance), since government hospitals are primarily for citizens. Practically:

- **Primary care**: available to expats for emergencies and some basic services.
- **Government hospitals**: expats need a referral from their insurance hospital, not from a government primary-care centre.
- **Electronic prescriptions**: dispensed to expats from network pharmacies via [Wasfaty](https://sehhaty.sa/) tied to private insurance.

For expats — the largest use of Mawid is booking appointments at private insurance-network hospitals connected to the Ministry's grid, not the major specialty government hospitals.
