## Details for each occasion

### The Holy Month of Ramadan

<EventCountdown eventId="ramadan" />

The ninth month of the Hijri calendar، the month of fasting, night prayer, and Qur'an recitation. It runs 29 or 30 days depending on the moon sighting.

**When it starts**: officially announced by the Supreme Court of Saudi Arabia once the crescent is confirmed on the night of 30 Shaʿban. It usually matches the Umm al-Qura astronomical calendar, though it may differ by a day.

**Recommended acts**:
- Fasting from dawn to sunset
- Night prayer (Tarawih)
- Recitation and completion of the Qur'an
- Charity and providing iftar for fasters
- Iʿtikaf during the last ten nights

---

### Eid al-Fitr

<EventCountdown eventId="eid_fitr" />

The first day of the month of Shawwal, following the end of Ramadan، a celebration of completing the fast.

**Its Etiquette**:
- Bathing and wearing the best clothes
- Eating before the Eid prayer (an odd number of dates)
- Takbir from the evening of the last day of Ramadan until the Eid prayer
- Performing the Eid prayer in congregation
- Paying Zakat al-Fitr before the prayer (one saʿ of the local staple food or its value)

---

### The Day of Standing at Arafat

<EventCountdown eventId="hajj" />

**9 Dhul-Hijjah**، the greatest day of Hajj. Pilgrims stand on the plain of Arafat from midday until sunset.

**Its virtue for non-pilgrims**:
- Fasting it is a confirmed Sunnah. The Prophet said: "Fasting the day of Arafat, I hope from God, expiates the sins of the year before and the year after."
- Abundant supplication, as it is among the greatest days.

---

### Eid al-Adha

<EventCountdown eventId="eid_adha" />

**10 Dhul-Hijjah**، the first day of Eid al-Adha. Muslims sacrifice animals to honor the tradition of Prophet Ibrahim, peace be upon him.

**Days of Tashriq**: Eid runs for 4 days (10, 11, 12, 13 Dhul-Hijjah). Sacrifice is permitted on any of the four days.

---

### Hijri New Year

<EventCountdown eventId="hijri_new_year" />

**1 Muharram**، the start of the Hijri year. **It is not a religious holiday** and no celebration is legislated for it, but it is recommended to give thanks for the year that has passed and to pray for goodness in the coming year.

**Why the calendar was adopted**: it was instituted during the caliphate of Umar ibn al-Khattab (17 AH) because it marks the beginning of a new Islamic era (the Prophet's migration).

---

### Saudi National Day

<EventCountdown eventId="national_day" />

**September 23 every year**، the anniversary of the official renaming of the state from "Kingdom of Hejaz, Najd and its Dependencies" to "Kingdom of Saudi Arabia" in 1351 AH (1932 CE).

**From its story**:
- King Abdulaziz bin Abdulrahman Al Saud began the reunification campaign in 1902 CE with the recapture of Riyadh.
- The unification of the Kingdom took roughly 30 years.
- On 17 Jumada al-Awwal 1351 AH (September 23, 1932 CE), the royal decree unifying the name was issued.

---

### Saudi Founding Day

<EventCountdown eventId="founding_day" />

**February 22 every year**، the anniversary of the founding of the First Saudi State in 1139 AH (1727 CE) by Imam Muhammad bin Saud bin Muqrin in Diriyah.

**Its significance**:
- It commemorates the three-century-deep roots of the Saudi state.
- It was officially decreed in 2022 CE by royal order from King Salman bin Abdulaziz.

## How the countdown works

- Updates every second in your browser (no server).
- Hijri occasions are computed using the official Umm al-Qura calendar.
- Gregorian occasions are computed using the Gregorian calendar.
- If the occasion is today, a "Today! " notice appears.
- After the occasion passes, the following year is displayed automatically.

## Religious note

The dates shown are based on astronomical calculation (Umm al-Qura) and may shift by a day depending on the official moon sighting for religious occasions (Ramadan, both Eids, Arafat). For Saudis, the official authority is **the announcement of the Supreme Court** on the night the crescent is sought.
