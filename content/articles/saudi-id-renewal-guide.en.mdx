The Saudi national ID is the foundational document for every citizen aged 15 and above, and any renewal delay blocks many banking, government, and legal transactions. This guide covers the statutory durations, the Absher steps, and when a physical visit to Civil Affairs is required.

## Current Statutory Durations

Per the Civil Affairs Agency, ID validity is set by age bracket:

| Age bracket | Validity |
|---|---|
| **15 – 30 years** | 5 years |
| **31 – 50 years** | 10 years |
| **Over 50** | 15 years |

**Renewal requirements** (source: [Civil Affairs via Ajel](https://ajel.sa/local/lwpwjj)): the online window opens when the card has **180 days or less** remaining. Renewing after expiry triggers a **SAR 100** late-renewal fine.

---

## Absher Online Renewal — Steps

Steps as of the current 2026 Absher interface:

1. Log in to [absher.sa](https://www.absher.sa) with your personal account.
2. Navigate to **My Services → Civil Affairs Services → Renew National ID Card**.
3. Confirm the personal data displayed (national address, name, marital status).
4. Review the Saudi Post delivery fee shown on-screen, then pay via SADAD. Issuance and standard renewal are free.
5. Processing and card printing take **3–5 working days**, then **2–4 working days** for shipping via Splonline to your national address.

**Before applying, check that:**

- Your national address in Splonline is up to date and active — the card is shipped to it
- Your registered Absher mobile is working — the shipping notification arrives via SMS
- Your current photo is still eligible — the screen tells you automatically

## When the System Requires an In-Person Visit

Five cases require a Civil Affairs branch visit:

- **First issuance** at age 15 (no online path)
- **Photo refresh** after 10 years or a visible change in features
- **Lost or damaged ID** — replacement requires a police report
- **Data amendments** like name or birth date changes (by judicial order)
- **Saudi naturalization** — first issuance in person is mandatory

For branch locations and hours, see the [official Civil Affairs site](https://www.moi.gov.sa). Most branches operate Sunday to Thursday in the morning; booking a prior appointment on Absher saves hours of waiting.

---

## Statutory Fees

- **Issuance and standard renewal**: free.
- **Saudi Post delivery**: shown on the Absher screen when the service starts, paid via SADAD.
- **Late-renewal fine** after expiry: **SAR 100**.

If the request is not completed for any reason, the delivery fee is auto-refunded within 5–21 working days.

## Common Renewal Errors

- **"Service unavailable"** — appears when the card has more than **180 days** of validity left; the online window opens only when 180 days or less remain.
- **"National address not active"** — your Splonline address is incomplete. Log in at [splonline.com.sa](https://splonline.com.sa) and update your data first.
- **"Photo needs updating"** — the system directs you to a Civil Affairs branch for a new photo. Uploading your own is not accepted.
- **"Card not received on time"** — track the shipment via Saudi Post using the SMS tracking number. If undelivered, visit the branch 15 days after issuance.

## Additional Questions

**Can I authorize someone to collect my ID for me?**
No. The card is handed personally to the holder via Saudi Post using the national address; delegation is not accepted for delivery.

**Can I keep using the old ID until the new one arrives?**
Yes, as long as it has not expired. The old one is invalidated once the new one arrives.

**Does the Tawakkalna digital ID replace the plastic card?**
Inside Saudi Arabia the digital ID is accepted at many entities (hotels, banks, government offices). But **for GCC cross-border travel it is not accepted** — the Saudi Passports Directorate requires the physical national ID card, valid at least 3 months at travel. Always carry the plastic card.

## The Saudi ID as a Regional Travel Document

An underappreciated benefit of a valid Saudi National ID: it works as a travel document across the **Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC)** — the UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, and Oman. Under the GCC ID-based travel agreement, a valid Saudi National ID lets you cross land borders (King Fahd Causeway to Bahrain) or fly to any of the six countries without a passport.

Key constraints:
- **The ID must be valid** — an expired card is refused. This is why renewal delays cost Saudis practical mobility, not just paperwork.
- **Children under 15** don't have IDs yet and always need a passport for GCC travel.
- **Some flights within the GCC** still require a passport for airline registration systems that don't accept ID scans yet.

## Digital ID Inside vs. at Borders

The digital ID inside [Tawakkalna](/en/tawakkalna-complete-guide) or Absher is broadly accepted for identification **inside Saudi Arabia**: government offices, banks, hotels, health facilities, and traffic stops.

**At GCC border crossings** (air or land), the Saudi Passports Directorate has clarified that **the digital ID is not accepted** — only the physical national ID card, valid at least 3 months at travel, works. Always carry the plastic card.

## Post-Expiry Consequences

An expired ID cascades quickly through Saudi life:

- **Banking**: many transactions freeze automatically.
- **Government services**: some Absher services become inaccessible.
- **Employment**: HR systems flag employees with expired IDs.
- **Insurance**: health-insurance claims may pause.
- **Property**: real-estate transactions won't complete without valid ID.

Renewal takes 5-10 minutes online. The cost of delaying is disproportionate to the ease of doing it — set a reminder six months before expiry and finish it early.
