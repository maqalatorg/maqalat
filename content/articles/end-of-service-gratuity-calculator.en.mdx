End-of-service gratuity (EOSB) is the most contested payment between employees and employers in Saudi Arabia — not because the law is unclear, but because many sources circulate an outdated or partial formula. This guide builds the calculation directly from Articles 84, 85, and 87 of the Saudi Labor Law and gives you an interactive calculator that shows the exact entitlement with a full numeric breakdown.

## The Official Formula, Article 84

Article 84 of the Saudi Labor Law (Royal Decree M/51, 1426 AH, as amended) reads:

> "When the work relationship ends, the employer shall pay the worker an end-of-service award calculated as half a month's wage for each of the first five years, and one month's wage for each subsequent year. The last wage shall be the basis for calculating the award. The worker shall also be entitled to an award for portions of a year in proportion to the time worked."

Three unchanging rules come out of that text:

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Service under 5 years** | Half month's wage × number of years (and fractions) |
| **Service over 5 years** | Half-month × 5 years + full-month × (total years – 5) |
| **Wage basis** | Last monthly wage (basic + fixed allowances) |
| **Year fractions** | Pro-rated daily (days worked ÷ 365) |

**Simple example**: 7 years of service, last wage 10,000 SAR.
- First 5 years: 5 × (10,000 ÷ 2) = 25,000 SAR
- Following 2 years: 2 × 10,000 = 20,000 SAR
- **Total**: 45,000 SAR

## The Article 85 Exception, Resignation

The key difference between employer termination and voluntary resignation is that the latter carries clear reduction coefficients:

| Service length | Entitlement on resignation |
|---|---|
| Under 2 years | Zero (no gratuity) |
| 2 to under 5 years | One third of the amount (33.3%) |
| 5 to under 10 years | Two thirds of the amount (66.7%) |
| 10 years or more | Full amount (100%) |

**Applied to our example**: if the same worker resigned (7 years, 10,000 SAR), they get two thirds of 45,000 = **30,000 SAR**. The 15,000 SAR gap explains why an amicable exit agreement is often better than an individual resignation.

---

## The Special Case, Article 87

Article 87 grants the worker **the full amount even when they leave the job** in three specific cases:

1. **Force majeure beyond their control** (chronic illness, external circumstances).
2. **Reaching the statutory retirement age** (60 for men, 55 for women under GOSI rules).
3. **A female worker leaving because of marriage or childbirth** — this clause was **repealed by Royal Decree M/46 of 1440 AH** and no longer applies; the case is now treated as a regular resignation.

This is the point where many outdated online guides go wrong, quoting the original text without noting the amendment.

---

## The Article 80 Exception, Forfeiture

The gratuity is forfeited entirely if the worker is dismissed disciplinarily for one of the offenses listed exhaustively in Article 80:

- Assaulting the employer or a supervisor during work
- Failing to perform essential contractual duties despite written warning
- Committing dishonorable acts or acts of moral turpitude
- Intentionally damaging the establishment to cause material loss
- Fraud in obtaining the job
- Unjustified absence exceeding 20 days per year or 10 consecutive days after a written warning
- Disclosing industrial or trade secrets

Only in these cases is the gratuity forfeited. Any dismissal outside them still obligates the employer to pay in full.

## Practical Questions

**Does unused annual leave count toward the gratuity?**
No. It is paid separately under Article 111 (compensation for accrued leave days at the last wage).

**What if the employer delays paying the gratuity?**
You may file with the Labor Office within one year of the entitlement date (Article 222). Delays may trigger additional interest or compensation by court ruling.

**Is the gratuity always in Saudi Riyals?**
Yes. Even if the contract wage is denominated in a foreign currency, it converts to SAR at the exchange rate on the day the relationship ended.

**Can gratuity be waived in a contract?**
No. Under Article 8 of the Labor Law, any clause conflicting with the law is void, even if signed before the law took effect — unless it grants the worker more than the law does.

**Does the gratuity apply to non-Saudi workers?**
Yes, without distinction. The Labor Law applies to "every contract under which a person works for an employer, under their management or supervision" (Article 3).
