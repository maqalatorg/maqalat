Filing a lawsuit in Saudi Arabia today doesn't require visiting the court. Through [Najiz](https://najiz.sa/), the Ministry of Justice's digital platform, the case sheet is submitted electronically, fees paid, hearings scheduled, and attended by video — all without leaving home. This guide walks the steps at a **practical level** for anyone who hasn't filed before, and for those wanting to understand the process before consulting a lawyer.

## Before filing — 4 checks

Ask yourself before filing:

1. **Have you exhausted amicable resolution?** Some disputes (especially labour and commercial) must go through **mediation** before court, otherwise the case is dismissed procedurally.
2. **Is your case within Saudi courts' jurisdiction?** If the other party is outside the Kingdom, you may need international channels.
3. **Do you have the original documents?** You cannot file a claim without initial evidence.
4. **Are you within the statutory period?** Some claims lapse (labour claims after a year from end of relationship).

## Saudi courts and their jurisdictions

Each case type falls under a specific court. Najiz routes automatically, but knowing helps:

| Court | Jurisdiction |
|---|---|
| **Labour Court** | Employment disputes — wages, arbitrary dismissal, leave, end-of-service |
| **Commercial Court** | Corporate disputes, commercial contracts, banking, insurance |
| **General Court** | General civil, real estate, judgment execution |
| **Personal Status Court** | Divorce, custody, alimony, inheritance, wills |
| **Execution Court** | Enforcement of judgments, returned cheques, bonds |
| **Court of Appeals** | Appeals against first-instance judgments |
| **Supreme Court** | Cassation on major judgments |

## Steps to file a lawsuit via Najiz

### Step 1: Sign in

- Open [najiz.sa](https://najiz.sa/) and choose "Sign In."
- You're routed to Nafath national single sign-on.
- Enter your ID, approve the sign-in from the [Nafath app](/en/nafath-single-sign-on-guide) on your phone.

### Step 2: Choose "Case Sheet"

- From Home → "Judicial Services" → "Case Sheet."
- Choose the case type (labour, commercial, personal status…).
- Read the **filing conditions** — some cases require a mediation certificate or attempted settlement.

### Step 3: Party details

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| Plaintiff | Your data — pulled from Absher automatically |
| Defendant | Full name, ID or commercial registration, mobile, address |
| Subject | Short sentence (e.g., "Claim for delayed wages") |
| Claim value (if any) | In SAR |

**Note**: defendant data must be **accurate**. A name that doesn't match the ID = case rejection.

### Step 4: Case details

The most important part, and must be **organised and clear**. Practical rule: story → demands → evidence.

**Recommended structure**:

```
First: Facts
- State when the relationship/dispute began.
- List events chronologically.
- Identify the moment the damage occurred.
- Note the amicable-resolution attempts you made and their outcomes.

Second: Evidence
- Attached documents (invoices, contracts, messages, witnesses).
- Each document with its reference number.

Third: Demands
- The specific demand in detail: e.g., "Order the defendant to pay 12,000 SAR being March and April 2025 wages."
- Additional requests if any (compensation, legal interest, case fees).
```

### Step 5: Attach documents

- Upload clear scans (PDF or JPG) of each document.
- Clear file names: "employment-contract.pdf," "bank-statement.pdf."
- Max size typically 20 MB per file.
- **Don't hide evidence**: withholding evidence that the defendant later reveals weakens you.

### Step 6: Review and pay fees

- Najiz shows a case-sheet summary — review carefully.
- Fees shown if any — paid via mada or credit card.
- After payment, your **case number** appears — save it.

### Step 7: Wait for the hearing date

- The court notifies the defendant electronically.
- Within days to weeks, you receive an SMS with the hearing date.
- The date also appears in the Najiz app on your phone.

## Attending the hearing by video

Most Saudi hearings today run on the **"Virtual Court"** platform integrated in Najiz.

### Preparation before the hearing

- **Quiet space**: no noise, no TV behind you.
- **Simple background**: a plain wall preferred.
- **Respectful attire**: as if attending court in person.
- **Good hardware**: HD camera, clear mic, stable internet.
- **Documents ready**: digital copies on screen, printed as backup.

### Joining the hearing

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | 10 minutes before the hearing: Najiz → My Cases → Case → "Hearings" |
| 2 | "Join Hearing" — video window opens |
| 3 | Approve camera and microphone |
| 4 | Virtual room appears: you, the defendant, the judge |
| 5 | Judge starts by verifying party identities |
| 6 | Each party speaks in turn — no interruptions |
| 7 | Hearing is auto-recorded in the court system |

### Hearing etiquette

- **Stand when addressing the judge** if possible.
- **Begin with "Your Honour"** or the judge's proper title.
- **Do not interrupt**: wait for permission to speak.
- **Direct answers**: no digressions.
- **Complete honesty**: lying in court carries severe penalties.
- **Do not mute your microphone without permission**.

### If the connection drops

- The hearing is temporarily suspended.
- Reconnect within 5 minutes.
- If the disconnect persists, the hearing is automatically postponed.

## When do you need physical attendance?

- **Criminal cases**.
- **Cases requiring inspection** (e.g., real-estate dispute needing site view).
- **Cases with multiple witnesses** sometimes.
- **Judge's discretion**: the judge may require physical attendance of any party.

Physical hearing is at the court itself. You receive a **notice of attendance** with room number and time.

## After the hearing

- **Hearing minutes**: published in Najiz within 48 hours — review for accuracy.
- **Multiple hearings**: most cases need 2-5 hearings.
- **Deadlines**: the judge may request additional documents by a specific date — don't be late.
- **Judgment**: appears in Najiz as soon as issued.

## Appealing the judgment

- **Appeal window**: 30 days from receiving the judgment (10 days in some urgent cases).
- **Method**: via Najiz → Case → "Objection/Appeal."
- **Attach appeal reasons** and new evidence if any.
- **Court of Appeals** reviews within two months to a year.

## When do you need a lawyer?

You can file a lawsuit yourself via Najiz. But **engaging a lawyer** helps if:
- The case has high value (over 50,000 SAR usually).
- The opponent is a large company or government entity.
- The case is legally complex (partnerships, bankruptcy, corporates).
- You've lost a similar case before.
- **You don't know the difference between "claim," "allegation," and "urgent motion."**

Fees vary — from 2,000 SAR for a simple case to a percentage of the judgment value for major commercial matters.

## Mistakes that hurt your case

- **Case without documents**: rejected immediately.
- **Inaccurate defendant data**: prolonged delay.
- **Vague demands** ("I want my right"): the judge won't know what "right" is.
- **Secretly recording the hearing**: illegal.
- **Speaking on speakerphone during the hearing**: not accepted.
- **Making a statement on WhatsApp with the defendant after filing**: may be used against you.
