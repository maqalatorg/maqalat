## What you're looking at

The tool shows today's date in Riyadh time using both calendars: Gregorian (solar) and Hijri (lunar, per Umm al-Qura). Each card gives you three things: **day number**, **month name**, and **month number** from 1 to 12. Below the two cards, a single summary line answers the most common question directly: "what's today's date?"

The Hijri day begins at **sunset the previous Gregorian day**, not at midnight. So if you open this page after maghrib in Saudi Arabia, the Hijri date you see is what many traditional almanacs would print for **tomorrow** in Gregorian terms. The tool follows Umm al-Qura's rule automatically.

A Hijri month is 29 or 30 days. A Hijri year is typically 354 days (355 in a lunar leap year). In Saudi Arabia, month starts are set by the Umm al-Qura astronomical calculation and confirmed by moon sighting where required.

## Why does Ramadan shift each year?

The ~11-day gap between the lunar and solar years means every Hijri month advances by roughly 11 days on the Gregorian calendar each year. Over about 33 years, Ramadan completes a full cycle through all seasons. A quick view:

- **2024 CE**: Ramadan started in March (spring).
- **2025 CE**: Ramadan advanced to late February.
- **2030 CE**: Ramadan is expected in January (winter).
- **2035 CE**: Ramadan returns to around November.

That is why an older Saudi may recall fasting in the peak of summer heat as a child and in the cold of winter as an adult — both are literally true, roughly 17 years apart.

## How this page computes the date

We use your browser's `Intl.DateTimeFormat` API with the `islamic-umalqura` option, set to the `Asia/Riyadh` time zone. Every modern browser (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge) ships with the same official Umm al-Qura data published by [KACST](https://www.kacst.gov.sa/) at the [official Umm al-Qura site](https://www.ummulqura.org.sa/). No server, no internet connection, no data ever leaves your device.

This means the tool is **day-accurate through 1500 AH (~2076 CE)** and does not rely on the approximate lunar-year formula that many other websites use.
