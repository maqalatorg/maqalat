Many job applications, university admissions, and government transactions ask for "official verification" of your certificate. In Saudi Arabia this goes through two different services depending on the certificate type, plus a completely separate service if your certificate was issued abroad. This guide explains which one you need and when.

## The two core services — and how they differ

| Criterion | [Mu'ahhal](https://jameah.moe.gov.sa/) | [Nataeji](https://nataeji.moe.gov.sa/) |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate type | University (bachelor's, master's, PhD) | General education (primary through secondary) |
| Who uses it | The certificate holder, for lookup and correction | The holder (to issue/reissue) and outside parties (to verify) |
| How an outside party verifies | Indirect — via a university letter or a certified copy from your account | Direct — a link or QR code, no login needed |
| Login | National SSO or a service account | National SSO to issue; no account needed to verify |
| Managing body | Ministry of Education | Ministry of Education |

## Mu'ahhal — for university qualifications

The idea: the Ministry of Education links a **unified data repository** to every body that issues university certificates (Saudi universities and some accredited bodies). You log in and see what's registered under your name.

### What can you do in it?

| Action | Detail |
|---|---|
| Look up your qualifications | Every university certificate registered under your name in the ministry's repository |
| Request a data correction | For a spelling error or an incorrect date |
| Add a missing qualification | Sends a confirmation request to the issuing university |

### How to access it

1. Open the [Jameah portal (jameah.moe.gov.sa)](https://jameah.moe.gov.sa/).
2. Select "Mu'ahhal Service" from the services list.
3. Sign in (National SSO or a service account).
4. Your registered qualifications appear — review them for accuracy.

**Important note**: Mu'ahhal is primarily a **personal lookup** tool for the certificate holder, not a public verification portal any outside party can enter without an account — unlike Nataeji. If a party (an employer, a university) asks for verification of your university certificate, the safer route is to ask your university's admissions and registration office for an **official verification letter** sent directly to that party.

## Nataeji — for general-education certificates

A separate service for pre-university certificates (secondary school and below):

1. Sign in to [Nataeji (nataeji.moe.gov.sa)](https://nataeji.moe.gov.sa/) to issue or reissue your digital certificate.
2. The digital certificate carries a **QR code**.
3. Any party needing to verify it scans the code or opens the verification link on the [Education Portal (edugate.moe.gov.sa/certificate/verify)](https://edugate.moe.gov.sa/certificate/verify) — **no account or login needed**.
4. The certificate's validity is shown instantly, with no physical stamp required.

This is the easier path if the certificate to be verified is from the general-education stage (for example, applying to a university abroad with a high-school certificate).

## Certificate equivalency — a completely different path

If your certificate was **issued outside Saudi Arabia** and needs to be recognised as equivalent to a Saudi certificate (for a government job, or for local graduate studies), this is **not verification, it's equivalency**, and a different body handles it:

- **Authority**: the University Certificate Equivalency Committee at the Ministry of Education.
- **Application system**: the [University Certificate Equivalency System (eqs.moe.gov.sa)](https://eqs.moe.gov.sa/) for checking application status; the actual application is submitted through the [Safeer 2 portal (safeer2.moe.gov.sa)](https://safeer2.moe.gov.sa/).
- **Objecting to a decision**: a separate objections system, also reached via eqs.moe.gov.sa.

**Note**: some unofficial sources attribute certificate equivalency to the Education and Training Evaluation Commission (ETEC) — this is inaccurate. ETEC is responsible for accrediting education programs and national exams (Qudurat, Tahsili); equivalency of individual certificates is the Ministry of Education's direct responsibility through the committee above.

## Do you also need diplomatic (Apostille) attestation?

Saudi Arabia joined the Hague Apostille Convention in 2022, which simplifies attesting official documents for member states via a single Apostille stamp instead of the traditional double attestation (Foreign Ministry + embassy). **To know whether your destination accepts digital verification alone or requires an Apostille in addition**, contact the receiving body in that country directly — requirements vary by purpose (employment, study, immigration) and by country, and there is no single rule that covers every case.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing Mu'ahhal with Nataeji**: Mu'ahhal is for university certificates, Nataeji for general education. Using the wrong service means not finding your certificate.
- **Assuming Mu'ahhal is a public verification portal**: it's a personal lookup tied to your account, not a link you share with an outside party the way Nataeji works.
- **Confusing verification with equivalency**: your Saudi certificate needs "verification," a foreign certificate needs "equivalency" — two different bodies.
- **Assuming ETEC handles individual equivalency**: its role is accrediting programs and exams, not equating individuals' certificates.

## When these systems fall short of your need

- **Training certificates (Doroob, Coursera…)**: not in Mu'ahhal or Nataeji — verify from the issuing platform itself.
- **Professional certifications (PMP, CFA)**: verify from the global issuing body.
- **Civil documents (birth certificate, marriage contract)**: other systems (Civil Affairs via Absher, Najiz).
- **Employment contracts and service certificates**: via the [Qiwa platform](/en/qiwa-platform-guide), not Mu'ahhal or Nataeji.
