The driving-licence medical exam is a **mandatory step** for obtaining a Saudi licence or renewing it in some cases. It's **protection for you and others on the road**. This guide covers everything: what's measured, where, cost, and what to do if you fail.

## What is measured?

The exam is comprehensive, covering several health aspects:

### 1) Vision

- **Visual acuity**: your ability to see objects clearly. Minimum 6/12 in each eye (with glasses if needed).
- **Visual field**: the range you see without turning your head. Critical for seeing traffic from the sides.
- **Colour recognition**: distinguishing red, green, and yellow (traffic signals). Severe colour blindness blocks the licence.
- **Night vision**: some centres test it.

### 2) Hearing

- **Basic hearing test** by device or conversation.
- Hearing aids permitted.
- Total deafness restricts the licence to specific categories.

### 3) General measurements

- **Blood pressure**: must be stable.
- **Pulse**: regular.
- **Height and weight**: informational.
- **General health indicators**.

### 4) Neurological and mental state

- **Epilepsy**: those with active, uncontrolled seizures cannot drive.
- **Severe psychiatric conditions**: may block (per medical report).
- **Substance dependence**: blocks.

### 5) Severe chronic diseases

- **Unstable heart disease**.
- **Severe uncontrolled diabetes** (risk of hypoglycaemia while driving).
- **Some severe respiratory diseases**.

These don't mean automatic rejection — each case is assessed individually to protect public safety.

## Where to take the exam

### 1) Via Sehhaty

The easiest and cheapest way:

- Open the [Sehhaty app](/en/sehhaty-app-complete-guide).
- Select "Services" → "Driving Licence Exam."
- Pick the nearest centre.
- Book an appointment.
- Attend at the set time.

### 2) At the driving school

Most major schools (Dallini, Saudi, Princess Nourah) have an in-school medical centre or partnership with a nearby one. Fees are usually part of your package.

### 3) At an accredited private hospital

- Mouwasat, Hammadi, Magrabi, and others.
- Slightly more expensive, sometimes faster appointments.
- The medical report is accepted directly by Traffic.

### 4) At government hospitals

- KFSH, National Guard, Prince Sultan, and others.
- Free or nominal for citizens.
- Usually requires a referral from a primary-care centre.

## Required documents

Before the exam:

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| ID or iqama | Valid |
| Personal photo | White background, licence dimensions (some centres photograph you) |
| Prescription glasses if you use them | Mandatory for vision test |
| Hearing aid if you use one | For hearing test |
| Prior medical reports if any | Especially for chronic conditions |
| Exam fee | Cash or card |

## Step-by-step exam flow

**Step 1: Reception check-in**
- Present ID and prescription (if you have a prior appointment).
- Receive the exam sheet.

**Step 2: Vision section**
- Acuity test (reading letters from a 6-metre chart).
- Colour test (coloured dot charts).
- Visual field (some centres).

**Step 3: Hearing section**
- Quick test by device or conversation.

**Step 4: Blood pressure measurement**
- Standard device, 2-3 readings.

**Step 5: Doctor interview**
- General questions about your health.
- Review of prior reports.
- Doctor signs the form.

**Step 6: Result**
- **Pass**: you receive a printed medical report.
- **Conditional pass**: licence restriction (e.g., "glasses mandatory when driving").
- **Fail**: with stated reasons, and the possibility of retake after treatment.

The report is sent electronically to Traffic — you rarely need to submit it on paper.

## When does the exam repeat?

| Case | Exam frequency |
|---|---|
| First licence issuance | Always |
| Renewal for Saudi under 65 | Not typically required |
| Renewal for Saudi over 65 | Every renewal |
| Renewal for a resident | Every renewal (typically every 5 years) |
| After a major accident | May be required |
| After new diagnosis | Recommended to inform Traffic |

## Common licence restrictions

Some exams produce a **restricted licence** rather than outright rejection:

- **"Glasses mandatory"**: must wear glasses when driving.
- **"Daytime driving only"**: for poor night vision.
- **"In-city driving only"**: for poor long-range vision.
- **"Automatic transmission only"**: for some movement limitations.
- **"Annual medical exam"**: for conditions needing follow-up.

Restrictions are printed on the licence and count as a violation if not observed.

## Appealing an exam result

If a medical centre rejects you and you believe it's unfair:

1. Request the **official rejection report**.
2. Go to a major government hospital (KFSH, National Guard, Prince Sultan).
3. Request a **counter-exam** as arbitration.
4. If decisions conflict, the case goes to a higher medical committee.
5. The committee's final decision binds Traffic.

## Tips before the exam

- **Sleep 6-8 hours** the night before.
- **No excessive caffeine**: temporarily raises blood pressure.
- **Wear your glasses** if you need them.
- **Have a light meal**: not empty, not full.
- **Relax**: the exam is routine.
- **If you're diabetic**: measure before to ensure a normal level.
- **Daily medications**: don't stop — tell the doctor.

## Common mistakes

- **Hiding medical history**: may be uncovered later and the licence revoked.
- **Exam at a non-accredited centre**: the report is not accepted.
- **Forgetting glasses**: may fail the vision test.
- **Missing the appointment**: may wait two weeks for another.
